Introduction

Welcome to the HomeComputer 6502! In front of you is a home-built computer whose heart is an old friend: the 6502 processor, the same chip that powered the Apple II and, in a modified form, the Commodore 64.

It was built in 2014 and 2015 almost entirely from components that were already available in the early eighties. Instead of a television set, it has a built-in display, and a battery makes it portable. That makes it a blend of the Commodore 64 and the first portable computers like the Epson HX-20.

After you switch it on, Microsoft BASIC is waiting for you – almost like in the old days. This manual shows you, step by step, how to do calculations, program, play games and make music with it.

About this manual

Chapters 1 and 2 get you up and running: switching on the device, getting to know the keyboard and display, and doing calculations in direct mode. Chapters 3 through 5 introduce BASIC, from your first program to arrays and games, and after that the SID sound chip. Chapters 6 and 7 are for advanced users. There you connect the computer to a PC, use the monitor and the disk drive, and get started with machine language. Appendices A through F are for reference: commands, error messages, memory, character set, keyboard and technical data.

Notation
