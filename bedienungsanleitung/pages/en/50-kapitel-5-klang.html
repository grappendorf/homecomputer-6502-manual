Inside the HomeComputer 6502 is the same sound chip as in the Commodore 64: the SID, in the later 8580 version. SID stands for Sound Interface Device. It has three voices that can play different tones at the same time. Each voice has its own timbre and its own volume curve. In this chapter you will make it sing: first single tones, then melodies, then multi-voice music. At the end, the computer becomes a piano.

The SID Sound Chip

All sound commands work in the background. The command starts the tone and returns immediately. Your program keeps running while the music plays. This way a game can play music and respond to keys at the same time.

Two controls determine how loud it gets. The control on the back (Chapter 1) sets the amplifier. In BASIC you set the SID volume with VOL, from 0 (silent) to 15 (loudest):

VOL 15

After power-on the volume is already set to 15.

Single Tones with SOUND

The simplest sound command is SOUND. It plays one tone on one voice:

SOUND 1,4389,8

This plays middle C for one second. The three numbers mean:

ValueMeaningRange
Voicewhich of the three voices plays1 to 3
Frequencythe pitch, as a number for the SID0 to 65535
Durationhow long, in sixteenths1 to 255
Instrumentoptional, the timbre (see below)0 to 7

The frequency is not given in hertz, but as the value the SID expects. For the tones around middle C these are the numbers:

ToneCDEFGAHC'
Frequency43894927553058596577738282868778

One octave higher the number is twice as large, one octave lower half as large. So the low C is 2194 and the high C is 8778.

The duration counts in sixteenths of a bar. How fast a sixteenth passes is set by TEMPO: the number of sixteenths per second, from 1 to 50. After power-on it is 8. A duration of 8 then lasts one second, a duration of 4 half a second.

A Scale

10 FOR I=1 TO 8
20 READ F
30 SOUND 1,F,4
40 SLEEP 0.5
50 NEXT I
60 DATA 4389,4927,5530,5859,6577,7382,8286,8778

Because SOUND does not wait for the tone to finish, the loop needs a SLEEP. Otherwise all eight tones would be started almost at once and you would only hear the last one. A new tone on the same voice replaces the old one.

Timbres

Whether a tone sounds soft like a flute or bright like a trumpet depends on its waveform. The SID knows four:

No.WaveformSound
0Trianglesoft, round, like a flute
1Sawtoothbright and strong, like brass or strings
2Pulsehollow to nasal, depending on the pulse width
3Noisenot a tone, but a hiss

The Instruments

So that you do not have to set everything individually each time, BASIC has eight built-in instruments. An instrument is a ready-made combination of waveform and volume curve. You append its number as the fourth value to SOUND:

SOUND 1,4389,8,1
No.InstrumentWaveformBehaviour
0OrganTrianglesounds at constant volume until the tone ends
1PianoSawtoothshort attack, then fades out
2BassPulseattack, then sustained
3NoiseNoisefor sound effects and percussion
4 to 7freeTrianglelike the organ, for your own designs

Without an instrument number the voice keeps its timbre. After power-on all three voices are set to the organ.

Changing the Waveform Directly

WAVE changes a voice's waveform immediately, even in the middle of a tone:

SOUND 1,4389,16:SLEEP 1:WAVE 1,2,512

The first number is the voice, the second the waveform from the table above. For the pulse wave you can give the pulse width as a third number, from 0 to 4095. 2048 gives a symmetrical square wave with a full, hollow sound. The further the value is from that, the thinner and more nasal it sounds. The next SOUND with an instrument number restores the instrument's waveform.

Envelopes

A guitar string sounds different from an organ pipe, even at the same pitch. This is mostly due to how the volume of a tone changes over time. This curve is called the envelope. On the SID it has four phases:

  • Attack: How quickly the tone gets louder at the start.
  • Decay: How quickly it then falls to the sustain level.
  • Sustain: How loud it stays for as long as the tone lasts. This is a level, not a time.
  • Release: How quickly it fades away once the tone is over.

With ENVELOPE you give an instrument its own envelope:

ENVELOPE 4,0,10,0,10
SOUND 1,6577,8,4

The first number is the instrument (0 to 7), then follow attack, decay, sustain and release, each from 0 to 15. For the three times, 0 means very fast, only a few thousandths of a second, and 15 very slow: the attack then takes 8 seconds, decay and release up to 24 seconds. For sustain, 0 is silent and 15 full volume. The example above turns instrument 4 into a bell: immediately at full volume, then slowly fading.

SoundAttackDecaySustainRelease
Organ (instrument 0)00150
Piano (instrument 1)0900
Bell010010
Strings, soft attack80158

ENVELOPE only changes the envelope. The instrument's waveform stays as it was. The new envelope applies from the next tone played with this instrument.

Melodies with TUNE and PLAY

Playing a melody tone by tone with SOUND and SLEEP is tedious. TUNE makes it easier. With it you write down a whole melody as text, in a kind of musical notation. PLAY then plays it in the background:

10 TUNE 1,"I1CDEFL8GGL4AAAAL8GL4AAAAL8GL4FFFFL8EEL4DDDDL8C"
20 PLAY 1

That is “Alle meine Entchen” (“All My Ducklings”). The notation consists of letters with a few additions:

CharacterMeaning
C D E F G A Bthe notes. The German H is called B here.
# after the noteone semitone higher: F# is F sharp
digit after the notethe octave of that note: C5 is the C one octave higher
O and digit 0 to 7octave for all following notes. At the start it is 4.
L and digit 1 to 8length of the following notes in sixteenths. At the start it is 4, i.e. a quarter note.
R and digita rest. The digit is the length of this rest only.
I and digit 0 to 7instrument for the following notes

In the example, I1 selects the piano. CDEF are four quarter notes. L8 makes the following notes eighth notes, L4 switches back to quarters. You may insert spaces for readability; they are skipped. Upper or lower case does not matter.

Caution

Always write B for the note H. An H and any other unknown character ends the melody at that point.

Tip

With the organ, identical tones in a row merge into one long tone, because it keeps sounding steadily. The piano strikes each tone anew. That is why the example uses I1.

A note text may be at most 47 characters long. The first number after TUNE is the slot, 1 to 3. Each slot belongs to one voice, so TUNE 2 plays on voice 2. PLAY knows these forms:

CommandEffect
PLAY 1play melody 1 once
PLAY 1 ONplay melody 1 over and over from the start
PLAY 1 OFFstop voice 1 only
PLAY 0stop all voices
PLAY 1,2start melodies 1 and 2 together

With TEMPO you make any melody faster or slower. TEMPO 16 plays twice as fast as at the start, TEMPO 4 half as fast.

Multi-Voice Music

Because the SID has three voices, up to three melodies can run at the same time. A nice example is a round: everyone sings the same song, but the second voice starts later. This is how “Bruder Jakob” (“Brother Jacob”) sounds in two voices:

10 TEMPO 8
20 TUNE 1,"I0L4CDECCDECEFL8GL4EFL8G"
30 TUNE 2,"I1R8R8R8R8L4CDECCDECEFL8GL4EFL8G"
40 PLAY 1,2

Voice 1 plays with the organ. Voice 2 plays the same notes with the piano, but first waits through four eighth rests, i.e. two bars. Then it comes in while voice 1 is already further along.

Noises and Effects

For games there are nine ready-made sound effects:

CommandEffectSoundDuration
EFFECT 1Shotnoise, fades quickly0.2 s
EFFECT 2Explosionnoise, a long fading rumble0.9 s
EFFECT 3Lasersawtooth, quickly sweeps downward0.2 s
EFFECT 4Jumppulse, quickly rises0.2 s
EFFECT 5Coinpulse, two bright tones (H, then E)0.6 s
EFFECT 6Bonustriangle, a chord arpeggio spanning two octaves upward0.5 s

EFFECT 9Falling bombsawtooth, a long downward sweep0.6 s

Effects always play on voice 3. A new effect replaces a running one. TEMPO does not change their duration. Voices 1 and 2 keep playing meanwhile. That way the music can stay in the background while things go bang. You can build your own noises with the noise instrument 3. For example, SOUND 3,20000,1,3 sounds like a short snare-drum hit.

The game gem.bas in the firmware project uses music and effects together. It is a good example to study.

A Keyboard Piano

Finally, the keyboard becomes a piano. The keys A through K play the tones C through C'. With 1 through 4 you choose the sound. The computer remembers what you play and plays it back when you press Return.

The program combines much from the previous chapters: arrays and DATA, reading the keyboard with KEY, subroutines, string functions, LOCATE and the LED. New are SOUND, ENVELOPE, TUNE and PLAY. You can also find it as examples/klavier.bas. If the drive from Chapter 6 is connected, load it with LOAD "klavier".

Preparation

10 REM KEYBOARD PIANO
20 DIM F(7),N$(7),T$(7),K$(4)
30 FOR I=0 TO 7:READ F(I),N$(I),T$(I):NEXT I
40 FOR I=1 TO 4:READ K$(I):NEXT I
50 TA$="asdfghjk":IS$="0124"
60 ENVELOPE 4,0,10,0,10
70 VOL 15:TEMPO 8
80 CLS:CURSOR OFF
90 PRINT "KEYBOARD PIANO"
100 PRINT "A TO K TO PLAY, 1-4 SOUND, ESC QUIT"
110 PRINT "ENTER REPLAY, BACKSPACE CLEARS";
120 KL=1:GOSUB 450
130 IF KEY(0)<>0 THEN 130

For each of the eight tones, line 30 reads three values: the frequency for SOUND into F, the name for the display into N$ and the note for TUNE into T$. Line 40 reads the names of the four sounds. TA$ contains the eight playing keys in the order of the tones. IS$ maps the sounds 1 to 4 to the instruments 0, 1, 2 and 4. Instrument 4 is turned into a bell by line 60. KL is the selected sound.

The Main Loop

200 REM MAIN LOOP
210 K=KEY(0):IF K=0 THEN 210
220 IF K>=65 AND K<=90 THEN K=K+32
230 IF K=27 THEN 600
240 IF K>=49 AND K<=52 THEN KL=K-48:GOSUB 400:GOTO 300
250 IF K=13 THEN GOSUB 500:GOTO 300
260 IF K=8 THEN GOSUB 450:GOTO 300
270 P=0
280 FOR J=1 TO 8:IF MID$(TA$,J,1)=CHR$(K) THEN P=J
285 NEXT J
290 IF P>0 THEN GOSUB 350
300 IF KEY(0)<>0 THEN 300
310 LED OFF
320 GOTO 210

Line 210 waits for a key. Line 220 converts capital letters to lowercase so the piano also plays with Shift held down. Then the key is dispatched: Esc quits, 1 through 4 (codes 49 to 52) switch the sound, Return plays back, the backspace key clears the recording. For anything else, the loop in lines 280 and 285 looks it up in TA$. If the key is found, P is the number of the tone. Line 300 waits until the key is released, then the LED goes out.

Playing, Switching Sounds, Clearing the Recording

350 REM PLAY AND RECORD TONE
360 SOUND 1,F(P-1),4,VAL(MID$(IS$,KL,1))
370 LED ON
380 IF LEN(R$)<46 THEN R$=R$+T$(P-1):N=N+1
390 GOSUB 700:RETURN
400 REM SWITCH SOUND
410 IF LEN(R$)<46 THEN R$=R$+"I"+MID$(IS$,KL,1)
420 GOSUB 700:RETURN
450 REM CLEAR RECORDING
460 R$="I"+MID$(IS$,KL,1):N=0:P=0
470 GOSUB 700:RETURN

Line 360 plays the tone with the instrument of the selected sound. MID$ picks the instrument number as a digit out of IS$, VAL turns it into a number. The LED lights up for as long as the key is held down.

The recording is simply a note text for TUNE. Line 380 appends each played note to R$, for example C4. A sound switch appends an I with the instrument number in line 410. Because a note text may be at most 47 characters long, the program only records while there is still room. That is enough for about 20 tones.

Playback, Ending and Display

500 REM REPLAY
510 IF N=0 THEN RETURN
520 TUNE 1,R$:PLAY 1
530 RETURN
600 PLAY 0:LED OFF:CLS:CURSOR ON:END
700 REM STATUS LINE
710 LOCATE 0,24:PRINT "SOUND: ";K$(KL);"  ";
720 LOCATE 3,0:PRINT "TONE: ";
730 IF P>0 THEN PRINT N$(P-1);
740 IF P=0 THEN PRINT "-";
750 PRINT "   RECORDED:";N;"TONES   ";
760 RETURN
800 DATA 4389,C,C4,4927,D,D4,5530,E,E4,5859,F,F4
810 DATA 6577,G,G4,7382,A,A4,8286,H,B4,8778,C,C5
820 DATA ORGAN,PIANO,BASS,BELL

For playback, line 520 hands the recording to TUNE and starts it. Every note lasts one quarter, no matter how long you held the key. The subroutine from line 700 writes sound, last tone and length of the recording to the display. In the DATA lines you see the difference once more: the sixth tone is named H, but in the notation it is called B4.

Keep Going

Experiment with the envelope in line 60 and listen to how the bell changes. Or map the black keys to the top key row: the notes would then be called, for example, C#4.
