With the VIA a display is quickly connected. This chapter attaches a text display with the HD44780 controller to port A. For the first time the computer shows something by itself.

The display

The HD44780 is the standard controller for text displays. You send it characters and commands over four or eight data lines, plus the lines RS (command or character) and E (enable, takes over the data). The 4-bit mode saves four lines: every byte goes in two halves over D4 to D7.

Our display has four lines of 40 characters and contains two independent controllers. Each drives two lines and has its own enable (E1 for lines 1 and 2, E2 for lines 3 and 4). The display in this book is an Electronic Assembly Blue Line EA W404B-NLW. We cannot read from it, because R/W is tied to GND. That saves a line, but after every command we have to wait a fixed time instead of looking at the busy flag.

Besides the control lines the display needs the supply, an adjustable contrast voltage and a series resistor for the backlight. With this display a resistor of 150 Ω fits.

You can also connect a display directly to the data and address buses. Then you have to take PHI2 into account as with the chip select of the RAM. We stay with the connection through the VIA.

The circuit

Port A of the VIA supplies seven lines: PA0 to PA3 go to D4 to D7 of the display, PA4 to RS, PA5 to E1 and PA6 to E2. The LED now hangs at PA7. It moves here from PB0, because port B belongs to the keyboard from chapter 10 on. The data lines D0 to D3 of the display stay open. The contrast voltage V0 comes from the wiper of a 10 kΩ trimmer between +5 V and GND. The backlight hangs through R11 at +5 V.

Assembly

First the VIA. Port A gets seven short wires with the names PA0 to PA6. The LED stays at PB0.

  1. Switch the power off. Put a wire into each of the pins 2 to 8 of the VIA (holes 5a to 11a) and label them PA0 to PA6.
  2. The LED moves: the wire from PB0 (hole 13b) to 27b moves to PA7 (pin 9, hole 12b). R10 and the LED stay where they are.

Then the connection for the display on a new breadboard. The header is drawn here as one row of 18 pins. If your display has a connector with two rows, take over the numbering of the module.

  1. Run +5 V and GND to a new breadboard and connect the upper rails to the lower ones there.
  2. Insert the header JP2 into holes 5c to 22c, pin 1 on the left.
  3. GND: pins 10, 13 and 18. +5 V: pin 14.
  4. The backlight: R11 from pin 17 (hole 21b) to +5 V.
  5. The contrast: insert the trimmer R12 into holes 26c to 28c. The left leg goes to +5 V, the right one to GND and the wiper in the middle through a wire (27d to 16d) to pin 12.
  6. The signals: connect PA0 to PA6 from the VIA to the wires of the same name on this board, as in the table above.

The program

The controller needs a fixed time after every command. The program does it in three steps:

  • Send a nibble: put data and RS on port A, set E high briefly and low again. The controller takes over the data when E falls.
  • Send a byte: first the upper, then the lower nibble. Then wait until the controller is done (about 40 µs, 1.5 ms for “clear display”).
  • Two controllers: the variable lcd_e remembers whether E1 or E2 is pulsed. Lines 0 and 1 belong to E1, lines 2 and 3 to E2.
  • The LED: PA7 shares the port A register with the display. The variable led remembers its state, and every write to port A carries it along. led_on and led_off switch the LED.

At power-on the display is in 8-bit mode. The initialization brings it into 4-bit mode and follows the manufacturer's instructions:

The program waits 2 ms after every command. That is slow, but safe. First the constants and the main program:

        .setcpu "6502"

VIA_ORA  = $7f21
VIA_DDRA = $7f23

LCD_RS = %00010000              ; register select: command (0) or character (1)
LCD_E1 = %00100000              ; enable of the upper controller (rows 1, 2)
LCD_E2 = %01000000              ; enable of the lower controller (rows 3, 4)

        .zeropage
ptr:    .res 2
lcd_e:  .res 1                  ; currently selected controller
led:    .res 1                  ; state of the LED at PA7 (bit 7)
tmp:    .res 1

        .segment "VECTORS"
        .word   nmi
        .word   reset
        .word   irq

        .code
reset:  jmp main
nmi:    rti
irq:    rti

main:   cld
        ldx #$ff
        txs
        jsr lcd_init
        lda #<;msg_welcome
        ldx #>;msg_welcome
        jsr lcd_puts
        ldx #0                  ; column 0
        ldy #1                  ; row 1 (the second)
        jsr lcd_goto
        lda #'>;'
        jsr lcd_putc
        jsr lcd_cursor_blink
        jsr led_on              ; LED on
        ldx #0
        ldy #2                  ; row 2 (the third)
        jsr lcd_goto
        lda #<;msg_second
        ldx #>;msg_second
        jsr lcd_puts
halt:   jmp halt

The initialization:

; initialize the display (4-bit mode, both controllers)
lcd_init:
        lda #$ff                ; all lines of port A are outputs
        sta VIA_DDRA
        lda led
        sta VIA_ORA
        ldx #40                 ; wait 40 ms after power-on
        jsr delay_ms
        lda #LCD_E1 | LCD_E2    ; address both controllers
        sta lcd_e
        lda #3                  ; $3 three times: make sure of 8-bit mode
        jsr lcd_write4
        ldx #5
        jsr delay_ms
        lda #3
        jsr lcd_write4
        ldx #1
        jsr delay_ms
        lda #3
        jsr lcd_write4
        ldx #1
        jsr delay_ms
        lda #2                  ; then $2: switch to 4-bit mode
        jsr lcd_write4
        ldx #1
        jsr delay_ms
        lda #%00101000          ; 4 bit, two lines, 5×8 dots
        jsr lcd_cmd
        lda #%00001000          ; display off
        jsr lcd_cmd
        jsr lcd_clear
        lda #%00000110          ; cursor moves to the right
        jsr lcd_cmd
        lda #%00001100          ; display on, cursor off
        jsr lcd_cmd
        lda #LCD_E1             ; from now on select the upper controller
        sta lcd_e
        rts

And the routines the main program uses to operate the display:

; A = RS and nibble: set data, pulse E high (RS and data stay)
lcd_write4:
        and #$1f
        ora led                 ; the LED (PA7) is kept at every write
        sta tmp
        sta VIA_ORA
        ora lcd_e
        sta VIA_ORA
        lda tmp
        sta VIA_ORA
        rts

; A = command (RS = 0): high nibble first, then low nibble
lcd_cmd:
        pha
        lsr
        lsr
        lsr
        lsr
        jsr lcd_write4
        pla
        and #$0f
        jsr lcd_write4
        ldx #2
        jmp delay_ms

lcd_putc:
        pha
        lsr
        lsr
        lsr
        lsr
        ora #LCD_RS
        jsr lcd_write4
        pla
        and #$0f
        ora #LCD_RS
        jsr lcd_write4
        ldx #10                 ; wait about 50 µs
@wait:  dex
        bne @wait
        rts

lcd_clear:
        lda #%00000001
        jmp lcd_cmd

lcd_cursor_blink:
        lda #%00001111
        jmp lcd_cmd

; X = column, Y = row (0 to 3)
lcd_goto:
        cpy #2
        bcs @lower
        lda #LCD_E1
        bne @set
@lower: lda #LCD_E2
        dey
        dey
@set:   sta lcd_e
        txa
        cpy #0
        beq @cmd
        clc
        adc #$40                ; second line starts at address $40
@cmd:   ora #$80
        jmp lcd_cmd

lcd_puts:
        sta ptr
        stx ptr+1
        ldy #0
@loop:  lda (ptr),y
        beq @done
        jsr lcd_putc
        iny
        bne @loop
@done:  rts

; wait X milliseconds (at 1 MHz, 1001 clocks per millisecond)
delay_ms:
@ms:    ldy #199
@us:    dey
        bne @us
        dex
        bne @ms
        rts

; switch the LED on and off: PA7 shares port A with the display
led_on: lda #$80
        sta led
        sta VIA_ORA
        rts

led_off:
        lda #0
        sta led
        sta VIA_ORA
        rts

msg_welcome: .byte "Welcome to the 6502 HomeComputer!", $00
msg_second:  .byte "This is line 3.", $00

Assemble and write it to the EEPROM like in the chapters before:

$ ca65 -o lcd.o lcd.s
$ ld65 -C first.cfg -o lcd.bin lcd.o
$ minipro -p AT28C256 -w lcd.bin

The result

After switching on it takes a moment, then the text appears. The LED lights up. In the second line there is a greater-than sign with a blinking cursor, and in the third line (the second controller) the last sentence. If nothing can be seen, turn the trimmer: with a wrong contrast voltage the display stays empty.

Note

If the display stays empty although the backlight is on, the contrast is usually wrong or the initialization did not get through. Then check E1 and E2 (pins 9 and 15) for pulses and whether the data lines are in the right place. If only the upper two lines appear, E2 is missing.
