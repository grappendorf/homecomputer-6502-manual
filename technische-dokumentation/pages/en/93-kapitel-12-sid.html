A computer of the eighties needs sound. For that the HomeComputer gets the sound chip of the C64: the SID. It needs a second supply of 9 V, which the build provides externally.

The SID

The SID (Sound Interface Device) has three voices. Each has an oscillator with four waveforms (triangle, sawtooth, pulse with adjustable width and noise), an envelope and a volume control. Added to that is a filter for all voices. It is addressed like a memory chip: 29 registers, all but four of which can only be written.

The frequency of a voice is a 16-bit value. With a 1 MHz clock:

frequency value = frequency in Hz × 16.777216

For concert pitch A (440 Hz) that gives 7382 or $1CD6.

Caution

The SID 8580 needs two voltages: 5 V at pin 25 and 9 V at pin 28. Do not swap them and never apply 9 V to pin 25. The older 6581 needs 12 V and has other filter capacitors. This book builds with the 8580. Check the voltages with a multimeter before you insert the SID.

Voices 2 and 3 follow above ($7F67 to $7F6D and $7F6E to $7F74) and are built the same way.

The envelope

A tone should not begin and end abruptly. The envelope shapes its volume. It has four phases: attack (swelling), decay (falling to the sustain level), sustain (holding as long as the key is pressed) and release (dying away). The bit “gate” in the control register starts it. With gate equal to 1 attack and decay run, and the tone holds at the sustain level. With gate equal to 0 release runs.

Our program sets attack to 0, decay to 9, sustain to 8 and release to 10 (so the registers contain $09 and $8A).

The circuit

The SID is connected to the data bus and the address lines A0 to A4, plus R/W, φ2 (the clock), /RES and the chip select /CS_SID. For that the decoder gets its fourth output: Y3 lives at $7F60 to $7F7F. Two capacitors of 22 nF sit at the connections for the filter (CAP1 and CAP2). That is the value for the 8580.

The output stage comes from the C64. The output AUDIO needs a resistor R13 (1 kΩ) to ground. Then follow the ferrite bead FB1, which keeps out high frequencies, and a low-pass of R14 (10 kΩ) and C20 (1 nF). The transistor T1 (2N2222) works as an emitter follower and can thus drive a loudspeaker amplifier. C21 (10 µF) separates the signal from the DC component. The supply of the transistor comes from the 9 V.

A small active loudspeaker, that is one with a built-in amplifier, hangs at the output JP8.

Assembly

First the decoder gets its output Y3:

  1. Switch the power off. Bring out Y3 (pin 12 of IC10, hole 9j) as /CS_SID.

Then the SID:

  1. Run +5 V and GND to a new breadboard and connect the upper rails to the lower ones there. Bring the 9 V separately to hole 5i.
  2. Insert IC12 across the channel, notch left, pin 1 in hole 5d (wide IC, rows d and h).
  3. Power: pin 25 (hole 8j) to +5 V, pin 28 (hole 5i) to +9 V and pin 14 (hole 18a) to GND. C16 sits between 9 V and GND, C17 between +5 V and GND.
  4. The filter capacitors: C18 between pin 1 and pin 2 (holes 5a and 6a), C19 between pin 3 and pin 4 (holes 7a and 8a).
  5. Connect the control wires to the connections of the same name. The table shows the pins. Pins 23, 24 and 26 stay open.

Finally the output stage on a breadboard of its own. It only gets the 9 V and GND:

  1. Run +9 V and GND to the rails and connect the upper ones to the lower ones.
  2. R13 from hole 5b to GND. The ferrite bead FB1 from hole 5c to 9c. R14 from 9d to 14d. C20 from 14b to GND.

  5. The wire AUDIO connects pin 27 of the SID (breadboard 12) with hole 5a.

The program

The program turns the keyboard into a little piano. In an endless loop it scans the keyboard and looks up the scancode of the key in a table. If it finds it, it sets the frequency of the first voice and the gate bit. The oscillator runs through attack and decay and holds the sustain level. When the key is released, the program clears the gate bit and the tone dies away.

The keys of the middle row are the white keys, those of the upper row the black keys, as on a piano. The key with the Z sits at the place where English keyboards have the Y:

KeyAWSEDFTGZHUJKOLP
NoteCC#DD#EFF#GG#AA#Bcc#dd#

The main program and the note values:

        .setcpu "6502"

VIA1_ORB     = $7f20
VIA1_DDRB    = $7f22
VIA2_ORB     = $7f40
VIA2_IRA     = $7f41
VIA2_DDRB    = $7f42
VIA2_DDRA    = $7f43
SID_FREQ_L   = $7f60
SID_FREQ_H   = $7f61
SID_CTRL     = $7f64
SID_AD       = $7f65
SID_SR       = $7f66
SID_VOLUME   = $7f78

GATE_ON  = %00100001            ; sawtooth, gate on: the tone starts
GATE_OFF = %00100000            ; sawtooth, gate off: the tone dies away

        .zeropage
tmp:    .res 1
code:   .res 1                  ; code of the pressed key

        .segment "VECTORS"
        .word   nmi
        .word   reset
        .word   irq

        .code
reset:  jmp main
nmi:    rti
irq:    rti

main:   cld
        ldx #$ff
        txs
        jsr keys_init
        lda #$09                ; attack 0, decay 9
        sta SID_AD
        lda #$8a                ; sustain 8, release 10
        sta SID_SR
        lda #$0f                ; full volume
        sta SID_VOLUME
loop:   jsr keys_update
        ldx #0
@find:  lda note_keys,x         ; look the key up in the table
        cmp #$ff
        beq @silent
        cmp code
        beq @play
        inx
        bne @find
@play:  lda note_lo,x           ; set the frequency and start the tone
        sta SID_FREQ_L
        lda note_hi,x
        sta SID_FREQ_H
        lda #GATE_ON
        sta SID_CTRL
        jmp loop
@silent:
        lda #GATE_OFF           ; no note pressed: let the tone die away
        sta SID_CTRL
        jmp loop
; keys (scancodes) and the frequency values of the notes C to D5
note_keys: .byte 1, 67, 65, 11, 9, 17, 23, 21, 31, 29, 27, 25, 33, 83, 81, 43, $ff
note_lo:   .byte $25, $2a, $3f, $64, $9a, $e3, $3f, $b1, $38, $d6, $8d, $5e, $4b, $55, $7e, $c8
note_hi:   .byte $11, $12, $13, $14, $15, $16, $18, $19, $1b, $1c, $1e, $20, $22, $24, $26, $28

Measuring

If you hold the key H pressed, you see a sawtooth of 440 Hz at the output:

Note

No sound? Check whether the SID answers at $7F60 (at the monitor: w 7f78 0f). Then the supply with 5 V and 9 V, the signal at pin 27 and the output stage, last the loudspeaker. If the tone hums, decoupling capacitors are missing or the ground connection of the output stage is loose.
