The computer needs a way to talk to you. In this chapter it gets a serial interface. For the first time “Hello World!” appears on the screen of a PC.

Why an interface?

In the old days you would connect keys and LEDs (or seven-segment displays) to the computer, later a keyboard and an LCD or your television. Hobbyists did not have a second computer for development back then.

Today you write the programs on a PC and transfer them to the circuit. With an interface to the PC we can send commands, query memory and registers and later even load programs into the computer. That way we do not have to rewrite the EEPROM for every experiment.

For this we use an ACIA 6551 (Asynchronous Communications Interface Adapter). It contains a serial interface and is addressed like a memory. On the PC side a USB-to-serial converter is plugged in.

Address decoding

So far the processor sees the EEPROM at all 64K addresses. Now it has to choose between several chips. The technique is called address decoding. We keep it as simple as possible:

  • The EEPROM is active only when A15 is high: from $8000 to $FFFF. An inverter turns A15 into the signal /CS_ROM, which is active low.
  • The ACIA is active when A15 is low. It therefore appears many times in the lower 32 KB.
  • A0 and A1 select one of the four registers of the ACIA.

The circuit

The inverter IC1A (pins 1 and 2 of the 74LS04) generates the signal /CS_ROM from A15. It goes to /CE and /OE of the EEPROM, which were tied to GND so far. The ACIA has two chip select inputs: CS0 is tied to +5 V, /CS1 gets A15. So it is selected when A15 is low.

The remaining connections: the clock PHI2 and the R/W line come from the processor, /RES from the reset circuit. The data lines D0 to D7 are on the data bus, RS0 and RS1 on A0 and A1. A 1.8432 MHz crystal Q2 with a 27 pF capacitor C8 generates the clock for the baud rate. TxD and RxD lead to the pin header JP1: pin 1 GND, pin 2 TxD, pin 3 RxD.

Pay attention to /CTS (pin 9): it must be tied to GND, otherwise the ACIA does not transmit. The inputs /DCD and /DSR are tied to GND as well, so the ACIA sees them as “ready”.

Plus a USB-to-serial converter (for example a module with the FT232 chip) and a fourth breadboard. You already have the inverter IC1A: it is the free gate of the 74LS04 on breadboard 1.

Caution

The converter must have 5 V TTL levels. A device with RS-232 levels (±12 V) would destroy the ACIA. A pure 3.3 V converter does not fit the 5 V of the circuit. Set a switchable converter to 5 V.

Note

Use a classic NMOS 6551 (Rockwell, MOS, Synertek). With the newer CMOS successor W65C51 the “transmitter empty” status bit is known not to be reliable, and the program of this chapter then does not work.

Assembly

First the inverter. After chapter 4 gates A to C of the 74LS04 are free. Use gate A on breadboard 1:

  1. Switch the power off. Run A15 from pin 25 of the 6502 to pin 1 of IC1 (hole 5a on breadboard 1).
  2. The output, pin 2 of IC1 (hole 6a), is the signal /CS_ROM.

Then the EEPROM:

  1. Pull off the two wires that connected /OE (hole 11j) and /CE (hole 13j) to GND. Run /CS_ROM from pin 2 of IC1 to these two holes instead. /WE stays at +5 V.

Finally the ACIA on a new breadboard:

  1. Insert IC5 across the channel, notch left, pin 1 in hole 5d (wide IC, rows d and h).
  2. Power: pin 15 (hole 18j) to +5 V, pin 1 (5a) to GND and pin 2 (6a, CS0) to +5 V. Place C9 between the upper rails.
  3. Tie /CTS (pin 9, hole 13a), /DSR (pin 17, hole 16j) and /DCD (pin 16, hole 17j) to GND.
  4. Insert the crystal Q2 between pin 6 and pin 7 (holes 10c and 11c) and C8 from hole 11b to GND.
  5. Insert the pin header JP1 into holes 21d to 23d. Connect pin 1 (21a) to GND, pin 2 to TxD (from hole 14c to 22c) and pin 3 to RxD (from 16b to 23b).
  6. Connect the remaining connections with wires of the same name. The table shows the pins.

Measuring

Before you send text, check that everything still works after the changes. A short program writes a byte into the command register of the ACIA:

        lda #%00001011
        sta $02                 ; ACIA_COMMAND

The picture shows the accesses. A STA instruction with a zero page address takes three clocks: fetch the opcode, fetch the address, write the value. Only in the last clock the processor writes, and R/W is low there. During the writes to $0002 and $0003 A15 is low, /CS_ROM is high (the EEPROM is silent), and the ACIA takes the value from the data bus.

Hello World!

Now the first real program. At init_acia we set the ACIA to 8N1 at 19200 baud: eight data bits, no parity, one stop bit. At write the text is sent character by character. Before each character the program waits until the transmit data register is empty (bit 4 of the status register). At read it waits for a received character (bit 3) and then starts over.

        .setcpu "6502"

ACIA_DATA    = $0000
ACIA_STATUS  = $0001
ACIA_COMMAND = $0002
ACIA_CONTROL = $0003

        .segment "VECTORS"
        .word   nmi
        .word   reset
        .word   irq

        .code
reset:  jmp main
nmi:    rti
irq:    rti

main:
init_acia:
        lda #%00001011          ; no parity, no echo, no interrupts
        sta ACIA_COMMAND
        lda #%00011111          ; 1 stop bit, 8 data bits, 19200 baud
        sta ACIA_CONTROL

write:  ldx #0
next_char:
wait_txd_empty:
        lda ACIA_STATUS
        and #$10                ; transmitter ready?
        beq wait_txd_empty
        lda text,x
        beq read                ; end of text
        sta ACIA_DATA
        inx
        jmp next_char

read:
wait_rxd_full:
        lda ACIA_STATUS
        and #$08                ; character received?
        beq wait_rxd_full
        lda ACIA_DATA
        jmp write

text:   .byte "Hello World!", $0d, $0a, $00

The two register values in detail:

RegisterValueMeaning
Command%00001011no parity, no echo, interrupts off, transmitter and receiver on
Control%000111111 stop bit, 8 data bits, internal baud rate generator, 19200 baud

Assemble as in chapter 4 (first.cfg can stay) and rewrite the EEPROM. Connect the converter, start a terminal program on the PC and switch the computer on:

$ picocom -b 19200 /dev/ttyUSB0
Terminal ready
Hello World!

Press a key and the message appears once more.

Note

Nothing arrives? Check one after the other: does the clock run at pin 27 of the ACIA (PHI2)? Is /CTS (pin 9) at GND? Are TxD and RxD crossed at the converter and is the baud rate on the PC 19200? Does the crystal oscillate at pins 6 and 7?
