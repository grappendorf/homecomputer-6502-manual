Now comes the “Hello World” of microcontrollers: a blinking LED. For this the computer gets another chip, the VIA 6522, and the address decoding is extended for several IO chips.

More than one IO chip

So far /CS1 of the ACIA is tied directly to /CS_IO. The ACIA therefore answers in the whole IO area, repeated 64 times in the 256 bytes from $7F00 to $7FFF. With several IO chips that no longer works. We divide the area into blocks, each with its own chip select signal.

A 3-to-8 decoder 74LS137 does that. It is only active when /CS_IO is low. Then A7, A6 and A5 select one of eight blocks of 32 bytes. One of eight outputs goes low:

Each block has room for up to 32 registers. The ACIA needs four, the VIA 16, and a SID later 29. The registers of an ACIA repeat eight times in its block. The addresses $7F00 to $7F03 stay the same, though, and the monitor program keeps running unchanged.

The VIA 6522

The VIA (Versatile Interface Adapter) has two 8-bit ports A and B, two 16-bit counters (timers), a shift register and four control lines. Every port line can be set individually as input or output. For that each port has a direction register (DDR): a 1 makes the line an output, a 0 an input. The output register (ORA, ORB) determines the level of the outputs.

Four address lines A0 to A3 select one of 16 registers. In the block starting at $7F20 they are here:

The circuit

The decoder IC10 gets A5, A6 and A7 at its inputs A, B and C. G1 is tied to +5 V and /G2 to /CS_IO, so that it only works in the IO area. /GL is tied to GND and makes the address inputs “transparent”. Y0 goes to pin 3 of the ACIA as /CS_ACIA, Y1 to the VIA as /CS_VIA.

The VIA is connected to the data bus, to PHI2, R/W and /RES. CS1 is tied to +5 V, /CS2 gets /CS_VIA. RS0 to RS3 are connected to A0 to A3. The LED hangs at PB0 through a series resistor of 270 Ω, its other side is at GND. The outputs of port B deliver enough current for an LED.

Assembly

First the decoder:

  1. Switch the power off. Run +5 V and GND to a new breadboard and connect the upper rails to the lower ones there.
  2. Insert IC10 across the channel, notch left, pin 1 in hole 5e.
  3. Power: pin 16 (5j) to +5 V, pin 8 (12a) to GND. Tie G1 (pin 6, hole 10a) to +5 V and /GL (pin 4, hole 8a) to GND. Place C13 between the upper rails.
  4. Connect A5, A6 and A7 (pins 14, 15 and 16 of the 6502) to pins 1, 2 and 3 of IC10.
  5. /CS_IO (pin 8 of IC7 on breadboard 5) goes to pin 5 of IC10.
  6. The outputs: pin 15 (Y0, hole 6j) is /CS_ACIA, pin 14 (Y1, hole 7j) is /CS_VIA.
  7. On breadboard 4: pull off the wire from /CS_IO to pin 3 of the ACIA and run /CS_ACIA there instead.

Then the VIA with the LED:

  1. Run +5 V and GND to another breadboard and connect the upper rails to the lower ones there.
  2. Insert IC9 across the channel, notch left, pin 1 in hole 4d (wide IC, rows d and h).
  3. Power: pin 20 (hole 23a) to +5 V, pin 1 (4a) to GND and CS1 (pin 24, hole 20j) to +5 V. Place C14 between the upper rails.
  4. Connect the remaining connections with wires of the same name. The table shows the pins. /IRQ (pin 21) stays open for now.
  5. The LED: run PB0 (pin 10, hole 13b) by wire to hole 27b. Insert R10 from 27c to 31c and the LED with the anode (long leg) at 31b and the cathode at GND.

The program

We set all lines of port B to output, switch the levels off, wait, switch them on, wait again, and so on forever. The delay loop counts 200 times 256 passes. Each pass of the inner loop takes five clocks. That makes about 257,000 clocks, or roughly 0.26 seconds between the changes. The LED blinks about twice a second.

        .setcpu "6502"

VIA_ORB  = $7f20
VIA_DDRB = $7f22

        .segment "VECTORS"
        .word   nmi
        .word   reset
        .word   irq

        .code
reset:  jmp main
nmi:    rti
irq:    rti

main:   lda #$ff                ; all lines of port B as outputs
        sta VIA_DDRB
loop:   lda #$00                ; LED off
        sta VIA_ORB
        jsr delay
        lda #$ff                ; LED on
        sta VIA_ORB
        jsr delay
        jmp loop

delay:  ldx #200                ; delay loop: 200 × 256 passes, about a quarter of a second
@outer: ldy #0
@inner: dey
        bne @inner
        dex
        bne @outer
        rts

Assemble it as in chapter 4 with first.cfg and write it to the EEPROM:

$ ca65 -o blink.o blink.s
$ ld65 -C first.cfg -o blink.bin blink.o
$ minipro -p AT28C256 -w blink.bin

Measuring

At pin PB0 you see a square wave. It changes between low and high about every 0.26 seconds.
